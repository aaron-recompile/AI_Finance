Trading on a centralized exchange

The other half of the market — order books, driven by API. We'll use two examples: OKX (crypto) and Alpaca (US stocks).

The one safety rule · this week's floor

Demo / paper only — never real funds

  • OKX Demo Trading (crypto) · Alpaca paper (stocks) — fake money.
  • API key = trade-only. Withdrawal off. Even leaked, no one moves funds out.
  • IP-whitelist the key; key + secret in env only — never a repo, never the LLM.
On-chain we said "testnet only." Off-chain the fence is: demo mode + a withdrawal-disabled key.

Method 2 · check your state

Query, and stream

  • REST — ask on demand: balances, open orders, a fill's result.
  • WebSocket — a live feed: prices, order-book, your fills the instant they happen.
Auth's three classic pitfalls (all real pain): ① IP whitelist · ② the timestamp + signature · ③ the required headers. Get these right and the rest is easy.
The concept · backtest

Replay history before you risk

Run a strategy over past data → see how it would have done — without spending a cent.
But backtest looks great, live slaps you: fees, slippage, and whether both legs actually fill. Always discount the backtest.
The highlighted strategy (one of many)

Spot vs futures = the basis

A dated future trades off spot. At expiry they must converge — the wall. Long the cheap leg, short the dear one, hold to the wall → capture the basis.
A clean, self-closing trade: the expiry forces convergence, no funding involved. (The perp / funding version is next week, on Hyperliquid.)
