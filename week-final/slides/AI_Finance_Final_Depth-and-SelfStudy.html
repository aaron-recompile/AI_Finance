The Map & Its Edges

Where this course stops — and where to go next.
An honest slide. This repo is an excellent map and scaffold — not an encyclopedia, and not a production manual. Here is exactly where the depth runs out, and the direction to dig.
The honest version

A map, not the territory

In seven weeks you built a working view of AI + Quant + Web3 — end to end.
But a pro on a quant desk, a core protocol dev, or a senior researcher would see four clear ceilings.
That's not a flaw — it's the design. Knowing where the edges are is how you cross them.
Edge 1 · DeFi

We stopped at the simple version

  • AMM: just x·y=k (Uniswap v2). No v3/v4 — concentrated liquidity, tick math, range orders, custom hooks.
  • Lending: basic over-collateralization + health factor. Not Aave v3 / Compound v3 — isolation mode, E-mode, dynamic rate curves, liquidation-penalty and on-chain risk-parameter modeling.
You learned what a pool and a loan are. The depth is in the risk math around them.
Edge 2 · Quant

Demo strategies, not alpha

  • Strategies were the classics — trend, mean-reversion, cash-and-carry. No slippage optimization, no order-book microstructure (L2/L3 parsing), no real alpha extraction.
  • Backtest & execution were skeletons — API wired up. Not low-latency infra, overfitting prevention, or real execution algorithms (TWAP/VWAP, done properly).
You can place an order through code. Making it profitable at scale is a different craft.
Edge 3 · MEV

We assumed a friendly chain

  • Week 3's flash loans and arbitrage assumed ideal on-chain conditions.
  • On real mainnet an unprotected arb is front-run or sandwiched by a searcher in the mempool — close to 100% of the time.
  • We never touched the dark forest: private RPC (Flashbots Protect), bundle construction, gas auctions (PGA), priority-fee bidding.
On-chain quants fight this every single day. It is the part a demo can't show you.
Edge 4 · AI agents

Wrappers, not deep AI-finance

  • Week 4's agent is tool-use / function-calling: wrap an API or contract as a skill, let the LLM decide and call.
  • We never went into deeper AI + finance — RL for dynamic market making, LLM sentiment on filings and on-chain events, or on-chain ML / ZK-ML prediction.
The agent is the driver. The models that give it an edge are a whole field below it.
Why

It's the design, not an accident

An intro, cross-disciplinary course

  • "Can code, new to crypto" — or "knows finance, can't write Solidity."
  • So: breadth and "it runs, you can see it" (the 5-rung ladder) over drilling one point to the floor.

Seven weeks

  • ETH/Base → Solidity/Foundry → DeFi legos → flash loans → agents → CEX → derivatives → x402 → Farcaster.
  • 1–2 weeks each. There is no room to go deep — by design.
Where to dig

Four directions down

If the ceiling is…Go deeper into
DeFiUniswap v3 / v4 — concentrated liquidity, ticks, hooks; Aave/Compound risk models
QuantMicrostructure + execution — L2/L3 data, slippage, TWAP/VWAP, honest backtesting
On-chainMEV / Flashbots — private RPC, bundles, the mempool game
AIRL / ML finance — RL market making, LLM event sentiment, on-chain ML / ZK-ML
Pick one. Depth in one beats breadth in four when you want a job on an infra or on-chain-arb team.
The takeaway

You have the map. Now pick a road.

This course gets you a full-picture view of AI + Quant + Web3 in a few weeks.
Professional depth lives one level down — along v3/v4, MEV, HFT infra, and RL/ML finance.
A map's whole job is to show you where to walk next. That part is yours.
