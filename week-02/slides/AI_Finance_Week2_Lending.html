Part 1

A lending protocol = an on-chain pawnshop

  • You deposit collateral and borrow against it. No name, no credit check, no court.
  • So loans must be over-collateralized: always post more value than you borrow.
  • Four actions, that's the whole surface: deposit → borrow → repay → withdraw.
Why over-collateralized? With no identity and no legal recourse, the only thing the protocol can trust is the collateral it already holds. If you vanish, it sells your collateral. Code, not a credit bureau.

Part 2 · this session's x·y=k

The Health Factor decides everything

HF = ( collateral value × liquidation threshold ) / debt value
HF > 1safe — nobody can touch you
HF < 1anyone can liquidate you
Liquidate = repay your debt for you, and seize your collateral at a discount. This one line is the core of Session 4, exactly like x·y=k was for the AMM. Derive it on the board first.

Part 2 · make it concrete

One position, three prices

Deposit 1 WETH · borrow 1000 USDC · threshold 0.8. HF = price × 0.8 / 1000.

ETH priceHealth factorStatus
$20001.60  (2000×0.8/1000)Safe — plenty of buffer
$1250 = P*1.00The knife's edge — liquidation price
$12000.96  (1200×0.8/1000)Liquidatable — bots move in
The liquidation price is computable in advance: P* = debt / (collateral × threshold) = 1000 / (1 × 0.8) = $1250. You know your danger line before the market moves.

Part 3 · live-run code/lend.py (~60 lines)

Read the code — three functions

  • deposit / borrow — the assert HF >= 1 inside borrow is "you must stay healthy after borrowing."
  • health_factor() — the formula in code; no debt → ∞ (always safe).
  • liquidate() — when HF<1: repay the debt, seize collateral at 110% (the extra 10% is the liquidator's bonus).
The whole arc in one run: open at HF=1.6 → oracle drops ETH to $1200 → HF=0.96 → liquidated, liquidator takes 0.9167 ETH (1100 USDC of collateral ÷ $1200).

Part 3 · sweep experiment · code/lend_demo.py

Fix the position, sweep the price — three things pop out

What you see

  • ① HF falls linearly with price — halve the price, halve HF.
  • ② Liquidation price is knowable ahead: P* = $1250.
  • ③ Liquidation isn't just repayment — a 10% penalty.

At $1200

  • 0.9167 ETH seized.
  • ~$100 more than if you'd repaid yourself.
  • That gap is what pays the liquidator bot.
Punchline: don't borrow right at HF=1 — keep a safety buffer. The penalty is the market's fee for cutting it too close.

Open a position → drop the price → watch HF cross 1

cast call reads HF (free, view) · cast send changes state (a tx). view vs state, again. When done: pkill anvil.

→ one setPrice →

  • That single line knocks HF from 1.6 to 0.96 — matching lend.py exactly.

No human decides to liquidate you. The rule is public and automatic; the bots are always watching. That is what "law becomes code" feels like from the borrower's seat.

Key takeaways

Walk away with these

  • x·y=k is the whole AMM; swap output = y − k/(x+dx); slippage grows with trade size.
  • Lending is over-collateralized — no identity, so only collateral is trusted.
  • The Health Factor decides safety; crossing 1 means anyone can liquidate you.
  • Liquidation is automatic and permissionless, with a 10% penalty — keep a buffer.
  • AMMs + lending are the composable pieces the rest of DeFi is built from.
