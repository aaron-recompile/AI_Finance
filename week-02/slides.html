Where we left off · Session 1's open question

You can transfer — but no one trades with you

  • Last session you could hold, send, and even call contracts to move assets.
  • But "transfer" isn't "trade." You want to swap ETH for USDC — who's the counterparty? Nobody is waiting.
  • A traditional exchange needs a buyer to match every seller (an order book). On-chain, at 3am, for a tiny trade — often no one's there.
The AMM's radical answer: don't trade against a person — trade against a pool of money that always quotes a price by a fixed rule. No counterparty needed, ever.
AI Finance · Week 2
Part 1 · the one line that owns the week

The invariant: x · y = k

pool holds x = 100 ETH y = 200,000 USDC → k = x·y = 20,000,000 put in dx ETH, pool returns dy USDC, keeping the product k: (x + dx)(y − dy) = k ⟹ dy = y − k / (x + dx)
Derive this on the board once, slowly. Everything this week is a consequence of this single line. Own this equation and you own the AMM.
AI Finance · Week 2
Part 1 · intuition

The pool auto-quotes — and the more you buy, the worse the price

  • Take ETH out, you must put USDC in — so ETH gets scarcer in the pool and its price rises as you buy.
  • You don't get one flat price; you slide along a curve. Big trade = you move the price against yourself.
  • No human sets the price. The reserves + one equation set it, block by block.
Slippage is not a bug — it's the price. It's the pool charging you more for eating a bigger share of its depth. We'll measure it in code next.

Part 2 · live-run code/amm.py (~70 lines)

Read the code — three functions

  • swap_a_for_b(dx) — the invariant in code. The fee stays in the pool → that's exactly how LPs earn.
  • quote_a_for_b(dx) — price without mutating the pool (needed to compute slippage before you trade).
  • slippage_bps(pool, dx) — mid price vs. the actual average fill you get.
The punchline of python amm.py: small trades ≈ no slippage; large trades slide down the curve fast.

Part 3 · R4 main track · who fills the pool?

Liquidity providers & LP shares

  • Someone has to put the 100 ETH + 200,000 USDC in. Those are Liquidity Providers (LPs).
  • Add add_liquidity / remove_liquidity + LP-share accounting: your share = your slice of the pool.
  • The 0.3% fee stays in the pool → k slowly grows → every LP's share silently gains value (no payout loop needed).
Same idea as your Vault → ERC-4626 shares: don't record "you own 100" — record "you own X% of a pool that grows." Fees accrue to the share price, not to an account.
AI Finance · Week 2
Part 4 · the other side of being an LP

Impermanent loss — a vending machine that auto-rebalances

ETH rises

  • Your machine's ETH is now cheap vs. the market.
  • Arbitrageurs buy ETH out of your pool until it catches up.
  • The machine sold your ETH on the way up.

ETH falls

  • People dump ETH into your pool.
  • It keeps buying on the way down.
  • You end up bagholding the loser.
Both directions put you on the wrong side: always selling the winner, buying the loser — the opposite of just holding. "You'd have been worth more doing nothing." That gap is impermanent loss.

Drive the curve, then keep these

  • Slippage is part of the price and grows with trade size vs. pool depth.
  • LPs earn fees but eat impermanent loss; being an LP is a bet that volume beats volatility.
  • No counterparty, no operator — a pool + one equation is the whole exchange.
