Slippage = how far you slid along the curve. A small order barely moves it (price ≈ 2000); a big order stuffs your ETH into the pool and pulls USDC out, so your average price keeps getting worse. The number on the screen ≠ a big order's fill price — the curve is.

② Impermanent loss: the other side of being an LP

Think of an AMM as a "self-rebalancing vending machine": whenever the price moves, arbitrageurs show up and always drag you to the wrong side — selling the winner, buying the loser. Drag to see it:

"You'd have been worth more doing nothing at all" — that gap is impermanent loss. It's symmetric (2× and ½ lose the same); it vanishes when the price returns to the start (hence "impermanent").
An LP's real P&L = fees − impermanent loss: high volume + small moves → LP wins; quiet + a big move → LP loses.
